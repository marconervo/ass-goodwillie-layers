Adams SS for the Goodwillie layers of spheres and suspensions

This tool computes Adams \(E_2\)-pages for all the \(p\)-local Goodwillie layers \(\mathbb{D}_{p^k}I(S^n)\), for a given sphere dimension \(n\), prime \(p\), and requested range. When a target dimension \(m>n\) is provided, it computes the Adams \(E_2\)-pages of the fibers of all the maps \(\mathbb{D}_{p^k}I(S^n) \to \Sigma^{n-m}\mathbb{D}_{p^k}I(S^m)\). At \(p=2\), all dimensions are supported; at odd primes, the dimensions must be odd.

Mathematical explanation

The Goodwillie tower of the identity gives a filtration of the \(n\)-sphere whose successive fibers are infinite loop spaces of the form \(\Omega^\infty\mathbb{D}_{r}I(S^n)\), for certain spectra \(\mathbb{D}_{r}I(S^n)\).

Arone and Mahowald [AM99, Theorem 3.17] proved that, after \(p\)-localization, these layers vanish unless \(r=p^k\). This holds for every \(n\) when \(p=2\), and for odd \(n\) when \(p\) is odd. The first two nontrivial layers are the sphere spectrum and a stunted symmetric-group spectrum. More explicitly,

\[\mathbb{D}_{1}I(S^n)\simeq\mathbb{S}^{n} \qquad \mathbb{D}_{p}I(S^n)\simeq\Sigma^{n-1}\Sigma^\infty\!B\Sigma_p/B\Sigma_p^{(p-1)(n-1)}\]

Arone and Mahowald [AM99, Theorem 3.17] also described the mod-\(p\) cohomology of these layers as modules over the Steenrod algebra.

At the prime \(2\), their result is

\[H^*\!\left(\mathbb{D}_{2^k}I(S^n);\mathbb F_2\right)\cong \Sigma^{n-2k}\mathcal A_{=k}^{\geq n+1}\]

A basis is given by admissible square words \(Sq^{i_1}\cdots Sq^{i_k}\), where \(i_j\geq2i_{j+1}\) and \(i_k\geq n+1\). The \(\mathcal A\)-module structure is computed by left multiplication, followed by reduction using the Adem relations and projection onto words of length exactly \(k\).

At an odd prime, for odd \(n\), the corresponding statement is

\[H^*\!\left(\mathbb{D}_{p^k}I(S^n);\mathbb F_p\right)\cong \Sigma^{n-2k}(\mathcal A/\mathcal A\beta)_{=k}^{\geq(n+1)/2}\]

A basis is given by admissible words \(\beta^{\epsilon_1}P^{i_1}\cdots\beta^{\epsilon_k}P^{i_k}\), where \(i_k\geq(n+1)/2\) and \(i_j\geq p i_{j+1}+\epsilon_{j+1}\). Again, the \(\mathcal A\)-module structure is left multiplication, reduced by the Adem relations and projected onto words of length exactly \(k\).

When a target dimension \(m>n\) is specified, the calculator instead computes the fibers of the maps

\[\mathbb{D}_{p^k}I(S^n)\longrightarrow\Sigma^{n-m}\mathbb{D}_{p^k}I(S^m)\]

Algebraically, this amounts to adding an upper truncation to the bases above. At \(p=2\), the basis consists of admissible square words of length \(k\) satisfying \(n+1\leq i_k\leq m\). At an odd prime, it consists of admissible words not ending in a Bockstein satisfying \((n+1)/2\leq i_k\leq(m-1)/2\).

This target-dimension mode is useful for studying suspension maps, including the single-suspension and double-suspension maps. When \(n=1\), it also gives the layers of the simpler Goodwillie approximation described in [Ner26, §4.3].

The calculator approximates these modules by finite truncations and passes them to ext-rs [CCB] to calculate \(\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal A}^{s,t}\). Before the calculation, the module is shifted so that its bottom degree is zero; the corresponding shift is then restored in the chart. The horizontal axis is the homotopy degree \(t-s\), and the vertical axis is Adams filtration.

By [Ner26, Proposition 1.2.5], the connectivity of the \(p\)-local layer is

\[\operatorname{conn}\! \ \mathbb{D}_{p^k}I(S^n) =(n+1)p^k-2k-1\]

The calculator therefore includes precisely those layers whose connectivity does not exceed the requested range.

Differentials and hidden extensions are not computed.
